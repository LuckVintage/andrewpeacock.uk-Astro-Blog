Back in 2023, I was working on a Minecraft Server, LU7 Creative. As with most Minecraft Servers, I needed to find a good anti-cheat system to ensure players couldn't use cheats and to prevent malicious players from causing damage. While researching, I came across Spartan, an anti-cheat that advertises itself as "The intuitive solution when it comes to anti-cheat protection." After reading the plugin's overview page on SpigotMC and some reviews, I decided to buy the plugin. On 19th July 2023, I spent €22.49 and bought the plugin. SpigotMC made it very clear that it was a one-time purchase and that future updates were not guaranteed. Spartan has existed on SpigotMC since July 2016 and was still being updated regularly, so I thought it was a good option. Having an anti-cheat that is up-to-date is very important, as hacked clients and bypasses develop over time, rendering older versions useless. As a result, I needed a plugin that still received updates.

LU7 Creative was launched in August 2023 and used Spartan as the main anti-cheat solution until April 2025. During the time Spartan was on the server, many malicious players joined and were not detected by the anti-cheat. There were also numerous cases of seemingly genuine players being flagged for cheats. I have been considering replacing Spartan with a different anti-cheat, but never got around to it. I had most of the detections disabled, to prevent genuine players from being detected and automatically punished. I was able to leave a few of the detections enabled, as they didn't seem to falsely flag innocent players. But at the same time, they also didn't flag malicious players either.

Towards the end of January 2025, the owner of Spartan, Vagdedes, announced that he wanted to sell the rights to the plugin. I didn't think too much of this at the time. As long as there weren't any major changes, I wasn't too fussed about who owned the plugin.

Just under a month later, the new owner, GRArthas, introduced himself, as well as announced some changes to the anti-cheat. I did not notice any changes on my server.

![Screenshot of Discord message](/images/spartan2.png#center)
[Click here to view a bigger version](/images/spartan2.png)

A few weeks later, the new owner announced that he was changing the name of the anti-cheat plugin from Spartan to Vacan. This was just a name change, so didn't affect me too much. However, I couldn't help but notice how similar the new name was to another popular anti-cheat, Vulcan. Vulcan is one of Spartan's competitors, and I know there has been some drama between the two plugins in the past. By changing the name to something so similar, I feel the new owner was trying to trick users into potentially buying Vacan instead of Vulcan. Even if this was not the intention, I feel naming the plugin something so similar to a competitor was not a good move. At the end of the day, it's just going to confuse potential buyers as well as previous buyers. I feel the plugin rename was definitely intentional. But either way, it didn't bother me too much.

![Screenshot of Discord message](/images/spartan3.png#center)
[Click here to view a bigger version](/images/spartan3.png)

On 5th April, GRArthas posted an announcement to let us know about his new business model for the anti-cheat. As part of this new business model, the price for the anti-cheat was lowered to €4.99, as well as introducing a subscription model for most of the anti-cheat checks. This meant, that instead of being able to use all the checks that the plugin previously had, I would have to pay more money to be able to enable them on my server. Only 'miscellaneous checks' were included by default. If I wanted to regain access to the Combat, Movement and World detection that were previously included, I would have no choice but to pay even more money. I was furious when I first learned about the changes. Why should I have to pay more money, for the same features that I had already paid for? While I have no problem with them changing their business model, I don't think previous buyers should be impacted by this change. At the end of the day, we've already paid for the plugin, which included all checks. Why should what I've already paid for be taken away from me because the owner changed their mind? It's also worth noting that the most important checks that pretty much any server would need, were put behind the paywall. Having just the miscellaneous checks and nothing else is just not an option for most servers, forcing buyers to either pay up for the checks or ditch the plugin and go with something else.

![Screenshot of Discord message](/images/spartan4.png#center)
![Screenshot of Discord message](/images/spartan5.png#center)

There was a lot of backlash regarding this change from the community, particularly from existing buyers. Myself included. Not too long after announcing the changes, another announcement was posted. Previous buyers were able to send an email to opt-out from the change, and keep all checks forever. Straight away, I sent an email and expressed my intent to opt-out of the changes.

![Screenshot of Discord message](/images/spartan6.png#center)

I assumed that was the end of that. But I was quite mistaken. The very next day, the anti-cheat was pulled from SpigotMC against the owner's will. I can only assume this was because the owner, GRArthas, broke some Spigot policies by introducing the subscription model. From what I can gather, SpigotMC is unwilling to restore the resource on its website. This is fair enough, if the plugin has violated SpigotMC's rules in one way or another, then it's understandable they may not want to restore the plugin. However, this also hurts previous buyers of the plugin as there will be no future updates to the plugin available on SpigotMC.

![Screenshot of Discord message](/images/spartan7.png#center)
![Screenshot of Discord message](/images/spartan8.png#center)

On the 7th April, the new owner of the anti-cheat, GRArthas, handed in his resignation and ownership was parsed back to the previous owner, Vagdedes. I was happy to hear this, hoping everything would go back to normal.

![Screenshot of Discord message](/images/spartan9.png#center)
[Click here to view a bigger version](/images/spartan9.png)

The plugin was renamed back to Spartan, and the new subscription model was reversed... Only to be replaced by a different one. 

![Screenshot of Discord message](/images/spartan10.png#center)
[Click here to view a bigger version](/images/spartan10.png)

The plugin is now going to be sold only on two platforms, BuiltByBit and Patreon. If you choose BuiltByBit, you can buy a 12-month license, with the minimum price being $21.99. 

![Screenshot of Discord message](/images/spartan12.png#center)

Alternatively, you can support the owner on Patreon for $4 a month. 

![Screenshot of Discord message](/images/spartan13.png#center)

Since SpigotMC, the platform I originally bought the plugin from, is no longer an option, that left me with three options: pay $21.99 to buy the plugin again on BuiltByBit, subscribe on Patreon for $4 a month to keep access and updates, or use the transfer command to move my original SpigotMC purchase over. Since I bought the plugin on SpigotMC, I decided I'd use this method. But to my surprise, they want me to pay another €12!

![Screenshot of Discord message](/images/spartan11.png#center)
[Click here to view a bigger version](/images/spartan11.png)

After everything that has happened, there's just no way I'm going to give them any more money. I don't care how good the anti-cheat is. I've already paid for it once, I'm not going to pay for it again. The message also doesn't make very much sense to me. "Fortunately, you have already paid 8 EUR of this amount over the years." I have paid €22.49, not the €8 the bot seems to be suggesting. Even if I did want to transfer it over and pay the €8, it's still a subscription. I would just be paying €8 for the first year instead of the full price of $21.99. At the end of the day, I do not want to enter a subscription. I made a one-time purchase for the plugin on SpigotMC, I shouldn't be forced into a yearly subscription.

Of course, you'd think previous buyers could just stick with an older version of the plugin before the subscription nonsense was introduced. But an older version is not good for anybody. Short term, you'll probably be fine. But a year down the road, chances are the anti-cheat is no longer going to detect cheats very well, as hacked clients improve and developers work out how to bypass the anti-cheat. But I can't even use an older version of the anti-cheat, as it would appear it has been remotely disabled in some way. I have the last SpigotMC version installed on LU7 Creative, and the plugin was loading just fine. But when the server automatically restarted at 2am on 9th April, the plugin refused to load. I tried setting up the plugin on a brand new, fresh server, and it also errored out. So I can only assume the owner, Vagdedes, has remotely disabled the SpigotMC version of the plugin. This has put the server at risk, with no anti-cheat installed. There was no announcement to say he was going to disable the SpigotMC version. This makes the situation even worse because I can't even use the version I paid for! When someone in the Spartan Discord mentioned this, Vagdedes responded that they do not support SpigotMC premium resources anymore, so interpret that however you like.

There is a lot that should've been done differently here. To start, the new owner of the plugin (GRArthas) should not have renamed the plugin to Vacan. Even if he did have his heart set on the name, having a plugin with such a similar-sounding name is always going to cause confusion, no matter the intention. I'm sure there are plenty of other names that do not sound so similar to their biggest competitor. Secondly, if GRArthas wanted to change the plugin to a subscription model, it should not have affected existing buyers, as he quickly found out when SpigotMC pulled the plugin from their website. Introducing a subscription-based system for a plugin that previously offered one-time licenses is never going to be easy, there is always going to be some uproar. But in this case, I feel it's fully justified. I think Vagdedes made the right decision in reversing the name change, the plugin never should have been renamed to something so similar to Vulcan, and I think Vagdedes never would have done something like this. However, I do think his yearly subscription should have been applied only to new buyers. I don't think it's fair at all for someone to buy a plugin under the impression it's a one-time payment, only for your access to get revoked unless you enter into a subscription. While there is no official confirmation that Vagdedes has pulled the kill switch to prevent the SpigotMC versions of the plugin from working, I think it's very suspicious. I am also not the only person with this problem, so I think it's safe to assume something has been done to disable the plugin remotely. This should never have been done. At the end of the day, the plugin I paid for has been taken away from me and there is no justification for this. If this wasn't intentional and was caused by an issue, an announcement should be posted to inform buyers about the problem, as well as steps to resolve it, if required.

I am not against paying for a subscription, I actually think it's a very good idea, but I can't say it's morally right to force one-time buyers over to a subscription. If I were a developer for a plugin which accepted one-time payments, giving the buyer access to future updates, and wanted to move over to a subscription-based model instead, I would make sure existing buyers were not negatively affected.

As a result of everything that has happened with Spartan, there's just no way I can, in good faith, continue to use the plugin. Even if I received Spartan for free, I wouldn't want to use it. The anti-cheat never worked well on my server in the first place. I don't want to pay more money to receive the same product. Over the 19 months Spartan has been running on LU7 Creative, there just hasn't been any improvement to the detections. But that doesn't change anything. Even if Spartan was the best anti-cheat plugin available, I simply don't want to enter into a subscription for an anti-cheat. This all comes down to my personal preferences. I know there are people out there who would happily pay for a subscription to use the anti-cheat. But I would much rather pay for a one-time license to use the plugin, which is what I originally did when I purchased Spartan. I never would have purchased Spartan in the first place, had it been a subscription. Vagdedes potentially remotely disabling the SpigotMC version of the plugin, especially without saying so, is unbelievable. Not only has it put my server at risk until I noticed, but he has locked me, and other SpigotMC buyers, out of the plugin they initially purchased. While there is no proof, I think this was done on purpose, to force people to panic purchase the new subscription version, as it's much easier and quicker to do that compared to buying a brand new plugin and setting it up. If true, this is a very scummy move from Vagdedes and completely changes my view on him and Spartan as a whole.

My experience with Spartan has been disappointing. From inconsistent detections to confusing name changes, to a business model that does not consider previous buyers. I understand that software development takes time and money, and I don’t fault developers for wanting to explore sustainable income models. But there’s a right and a wrong way to do that, and disregarding loyal buyers who paid upfront isn’t it. I bought Spartan under the impression I would not need to buy the plugin again. I no longer feel in confident relying on Spartan to protect my server or its players. After the plugin disabled itself, Vagdedes decided on my next steps for me. I will be purchasing a copy of Vulcan, Spartan's main competitor. At what point did developers forget that the foundation of any successful product is its users?
